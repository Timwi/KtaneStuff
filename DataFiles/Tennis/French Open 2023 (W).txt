Defending champion [[Iga Świątek]]<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2022/jun/04/iga-swiatek-reigns-at-french-open-again-after-swatting-coco-gauff-aside-tennis|title=Iga Swiatek reigns at French Open again after swatting Coco Gauff aside|date=2022-06-04|work=Guardian|accessdate=2022-06-07}}</ref> defeated [[Karolína Muchová]] in the final, 6–2, 5–7, 6–4 to win the women's singles tennis title at the [[2023 French Open]]. It was her third [[French Open]] title and fourth [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] title overall.<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2023/jun/10/tennis-french-open-final-iga-swiatek-karolina-muchova|title= Swiatek digs deep to overcome Muchova in thrilling French Open final|date=10 June 2023|work=Guardian|accessdate=15 June 2023}}</ref>
Świątek was the third woman in the [[Open Era]] (after [[Monica Seles]] and [[Naomi Osaka]]) to win her first four major finals, and the youngest to win four majors since [[Serena Williams]] in 2002.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mesic |first=Dzevad |date=2023-06-10 |title=Iga Swiatek, 22, youngest player since Serena Williams to win four Grand Slams |url=https://www.tennisworldusa.org/tennis/news/Serena_Williams/133770/iga-swiatek-22-youngest-player-since-serena-williams-to-win-four-grand-slams/ |access-date=2023-06-11 |website=Tennis World USA |language=en}}</ref> She was the first player to defend the French Open title since [[Justine Henin]] in [[2007 French Open – Women's singles|2007]], and the first to defend any major title since Williams at the [[2016 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|2016 Wimbledon Championships]]. Świątek retained the [[List of WTA number 1 ranked singles tennis players|world No. 1 singles ranking]] after [[Aryna Sabalenka]] lost in the semifinals.<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.wtatennis.com/news/3487350/iga-swiatek-s-no-1-ranking-on-the-line-at-the-french-open|title=Iga Swiatek's No.1 ranking on the line at the French Open|date=2023-05-25|website=wtatennis.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/3487350/iga-swiatek-s-no-1-ranking-on-the-line-at-the-french-open|title=Iga Swiatek's No.1 ranking on the line at the French Open|date=25 May 2023|publisher=[[Women's Tennis Association]]|last=Nguyen|first=Courtney}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Tennis.com |title=Who will be No. 1 after Roland Garros? The four men and two women who can do it |url=https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/no-1-at-roland-garros-alcaraz-swiatek-medvedev-djokovic-tsitsipas-sabalenka |access-date=2023-06-04 |website=Tennis.com |language=en}}</ref>

Muchová reached the final after being a match point down in the third set of their semifinal clash with World No. 2 Sabalenka.<ref>{{Cite web |last=wtatennis.com |title=Muchova topples Sabalenka at French Open; makes first Grand Slam final |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/3532701/muchova-topples-sabalenka-at-french-open-makes-first-grand-slam-final |access-date=2025-05-01 |website=wtatennis.com |language=en}}</ref>

[[Beatriz Haddad Maia]] was the first Brazilian woman in the Open Era to reach the semifinals, and the first at any major since [[Maria Bueno]] at the [[1968 US Open – Women's singles|1968 US Open]]. By reaching the semifinals, Haddad Maia entered the top 10 of the [[WTA rankings]] for the first time, the first Brazilian woman to do so.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Beatriz Haddad Maia upsets Ons Jabeur to reach French Open semifinals|url=https://apnews.com/article/french-open-roland-garros-quarterfinals-e11719b41f1a3c3aa97eea23bb2a8e83 |date=2023-06-07 |website=apnews.com |language=en}}</ref>

12 of the 32 seeds reached the third round, the fewest since the French Open's draw was increased to 32 seeds in 2002.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.espn.com/tennis/story/_/id/37779787/no-3-jessica-pegula-becomes-highest-ranked-woman-fall?platform=amp|date=2 June 2023|title=No. 3 Jessica Pegula becomes highest-ranked woman to fall}}</ref> [[Elina Avanesyan]] was the first lucky loser to reach the fourth round since [[Nicole Jagerman]] in [[1988 French Open – Women's singles|1988]], and the first at any major since [[María José Gaidano]] at the [[1993 US Open – Women's singles|1993 US Open]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Avanesyan becomes first lucky loser in French Open last 16 since 1988 |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/3528355/avanesyan-becomes-first-lucky-loser-in-french-open-last-16-since-1988 |access-date=2023-06-04 |website=Women's Tennis Association}}</ref> Ranked No. 333 in the world, [[Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova]] was the lowest-ranked player to reach the French Open quarterfinals. With world No. 192 [[Elina Svitolina]] also reaching the quarterfinals, this marked the first time that two players ranked outside the top 150 in the world reached the quarterfinals at the same major.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://twitter.com/OptaAce/status/1665405229918035971|title=OptaAce on Twitter|date=4 June 2023|accessdate=11 June 2023}}</ref>
