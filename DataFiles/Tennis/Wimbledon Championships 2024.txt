Defending champion [[Carlos Alcaraz]]<ref>{{cite news |last1=Cambers |first1=Simon |title=Wimbledon a 'dream come true,' says Carlos Alcaraz after stunning victory |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2023/jul/16/wimbledon-a-dream-come-true-says-carlos-alcaraz-after-stunning-victory |access-date=24 June 2024 |work=The Guardian |date=16 July 2023}}</ref> defeated [[Novak Djokovic]] in [[2023 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles|a rematch]] of the previous year's final, 6–2, 6–2, 7–6<sup>(7–4)</sup> to win the gentlemen's singles tennis title at the [[2024 Wimbledon Championships]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/tennis/articles/c8864j5815go|title=Alcaraz crushes Djokovic to retain Wimbledon title|date=14 July 2024 |publisher=BBC Sport|access-date=14 July 2024}}</ref> It was his second [[Wimbledon Championships|Wimbledon]] title and fourth [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] title overall, becoming the [[List of Grand Slam men's singles champions#Two titles|sixth]] and youngest man in the [[Open Era]] to complete the [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Other concepts|Channel Slam]]. Alcaraz was the second man in the Open Era, after [[Roger Federer]], to win his first four major finals, and the first man outside of the [[Big Three (tennis)|Big Three]] to defend a major title since [[Andre Agassi]] at the [[2001 Australian Open – Men's singles|2001 Australian Open]].{{citation needed |date=September 2024}}

The tournament featured 37 five-set matches, the most at a major in the Open Era, breaking the previous record of 35 jointly held by the [[1983 US Open – Men's singles|1983 US Open]] and [[2024 Australian Open – Men's singles|2024 Australian Open]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.puntodebreak.com/en/2024/07/09/record-wimbledon-grand-slam-tournament-with-the-most-5-set-matches-in-history|title=Record Wimbledon: Grand Slam tournament with the most 5-set matches in history.|publisher=Puntodebreak.com|date=9 July 2024|access-date=10 July 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/medvedev-sinner-clash-clinches-open-era-record-36th-five-set-match-grand-slam-event|title=Medvedev-Sinner clash clinches Open Era record 36th five-set match in a major event|publisher=Tennis.com|date=9 July 2024|access-date=10 July 2024}}</ref> [[Giovanni Mpetshi Perricard]] was the first lucky loser to reach the fourth round since [[Dick Norman]] in [[1995 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles|1995]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/how-giovanni-mpetshi-perricard-became-the-story-of-wimbledon|title=How Giovanni Mpetshi Perricard became the story of Wimbledon|publisher=Tennis.com|access-date=8 July 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240710203330/https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/how-giovanni-mpetshi-perricard-became-the-story-of-wimbledon |archive-date=10 July 2024}}</ref>
