[[File:Novak Djoković French Open 2023 (cropped).jpg|thumb|[[Novak Djokovic]] with the [[Coupe des Mousquetaires]]]]
[[Novak Djokovic]] defeated [[Casper Ruud]] in the final, 7–6<sup>(7–1)</sup>, 6–3, 7–5 to win the men's singles tennis title at the [[2023 French Open]]. It was his third [[French Open]] title and record-breaking 23rd men's singles [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] title overall, surpassing [[Rafael Nadal]]'s [[List of Grand Slam men's singles champions#Champions list|all-time record]].<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2023/jun/11/novak-djokovic-casper-ruud-french-open-23-grand-slam-titles-tennis|title= Novak Djokovic reaches record 23 grand slam titles after French Open final win|date=11 June 2023|work=Guardian|accessdate=15 June 2023}}</ref> Djokovic became the first man to achieve a [[List of Grand Slam men's singles champions#Career Grand Slam|triple career Grand Slam]] in singles, and the oldest champion in French Open history at the age of 36 years and 20 days.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2023-06-11 |title=Novak Djokovic wins French Open for record 23rd Grand Slam title |url=https://sportsnaut.com/novak-djokovic-wins-french-open-for-record-23rd-grand-slam-title/ |access-date=2023-06-11 |website=Sportsnaut |language=en-US}}</ref> 

Nadal was the reigning champion,<ref>{{cite web|last=Abulleil|first=Reem|date=2022-06-05|title=It's Grand Slam No.22 for Rafa|url=https://www.rolandgarros.com/en-us/article/rg2022-nadal-ruud-final-report|access-date=2023-05-18|website=Roland-Garros}}</ref> but withdrew due to a left hip injury. This was the first time the 14-time champion missed the tournament since his debut in [[2005 French Open – Men's singles|2005]].<ref>{{cite web|date=2023-05-18|title= Nadal Withdraws From Roland Garros, Hints 2024 Season May Be His Last |url= https://www.atptour.com/en/news/nadal-roland-garros-2023-withdrawal |access-date=2023-05-18|website=ATPTour.com}}</ref> With his withdrawal, Nadal fell out of the top 100 of the [[ATP rankings]] for the first time since 2003.<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/five-ramifications-of-rafael-nadal-s-roland-garros-withdrawal |title= Five ramifications of Rafael Nadal's Roland Garros withdrawal|date=18 May 2023}}</ref>

By winning the title, Djokovic reclaimed the [[List of ATP number 1 ranked singles tennis players|world No. 1 singles ranking]] from [[Carlos Alcaraz]]; [[Daniil Medvedev]] and [[Stefanos Tsitsipas]] were also in contention for the top position.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/no-1-at-roland-garros-alcaraz-swiatek-medvedev-djokovic-tsitsipas-sabalenka | title=Who will be No. 1 after Roland Garros? The four men and two women who can do it }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://firstsportz.com/tennis-news-carlos-alcaraz-daniil-medvedev-or-novak-djokovic-scenarios-to-leave-paris-as-number-1-in-the-atp-ranking-post-french-open-2023/ | title=Carlos Alcaraz, Daniil Medvedev or Novak Djokovic: Scenarios to leave Paris as number 1 in the ATP ranking post French Open 2023 | date=22 May 2023 }}</ref> Medvedev's loss to [[Thiago Seyboth Wild]] in the first round marked the first time since [[2000 French Open – Men's singles|2000]] that the second seed (then [[Pete Sampras]]) lost in the opening round. This was the first major since the [[2003 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles|2003 Wimbledon Championships]] where neither of the top two seeds were members of the [[Big Three (tennis)#Big Four era|Big Four]].
