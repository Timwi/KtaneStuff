{{further|2022 French Open}}

[[Iga Świątek]] defeated [[Coco Gauff]] in the final, 6–1, 6–3 to win the women's singles tennis title at the [[2022 French Open]].<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2022/jun/04/iga-swiatek-reigns-at-french-open-again-after-swatting-coco-gauff-aside-tennis|title=Iga Swiatek reigns at French Open again after swatting Coco Gauff aside|date=4 June 2022|work=Guardian|accessdate=7 June 2022}}</ref>
It was her second [[French Open]] title and second [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] title overall. With the win, Świątek extended her winning streak to 35 matches (dating to the [[2022 Qatar Total Open – Singles|Qatar Open]] in February).<ref>{{Cite web|title=Iga Swiatek winning streak: How world No. 1's unbeaten run compares to Serena Williams, others in tennis history|url=https://www.sportingnews.com/us/tennis/news/iga-swiatek-winning-streak-how-world-no-1s-unbeaten-run-compares-serena-williams-others-tennis-history/kkdv6yjbugg4asv3oy1hpb2h|first=Joshua|last=Thomas|date=3 June 2022|access-date=5 June 2022|website=rolandgarros.com}}</ref> Świątek was the youngest winner of multiple majors since [[Maria Sharapova]] in 2006.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |date=2022-06-04 |title=Flawless Iga Swiatek sweeps to Roland Garros title, conquers Coco Gauff in final |url=https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/flawless-iga-swiatek-sweeps-to-roland-garros-title-conquers-coco-gauff-in-final |access-date=2022-06-04 |website=Tennis.com |language=en}}</ref>

[[Barbora Krejčíková]] was the defending champion,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/57455312|title=French Open: Barbora Krejcikova wins first Grand Slam singles title in Paris|first=Jonathan|last=Jurejko|date=12 June 2021|publisher=[[BBC]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220124102445/https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/57455312|archive-date=24 January 2022|access-date=13 April 2022}}</ref> but lost in the first round to [[Diane Parry]]. This marked only the third time in French Open history that the defending champion lost in the first round (after [[Anastasia Myskina]] in [[2005 French Open – Women's singles|2005]] and [[Jeļena Ostapenko]] in [[2018 French Open – Women's singles|2018]]), and the record 15th consecutive unsuccessful French Open women's singles title defense since [[2007 French Open – Women's singles|2007]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Parry pounces in Paris|url=https://www.rolandgarros.com/en-us/article/rg2022-diane-parry-upsets-barbora-krejcikova-r1|first=Chris|last=Oddo|date=23 May 2022|access-date=23 May 2022|website=rolandgarros.com}}</ref>

This was the first edition of the French Open since [[2004 French Open – Women's singles|2004]] to feature three Americans in the quarterfinals: Gauff, [[Jessica Pegula]], and [[Sloane Stephens]].<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.nbcchicago.com/news/sports/here-are-the-remaining-americans-in-the-2022-french-open/2845481/|title=Here Are the Remaining Americans in the 2022 French Open|publisher=[[WMAQ-TV]]|last=Elbaba|first=Julia|date=30 May 2022|access-date=31 May 2022}}</ref> Gauff was the youngest major finalist since Sharapova at the [[2004 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|2004 Wimbledon Championships]], and the youngest French Open finalist since [[Kim Clijsters]] in [[2001 French Open – Women's singles|2001]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://bleacherreport.com/articles/10037480-coco-gauff-beats-martina-trevisan-to-clinch-spot-in-2022-french-open-womens-final|title=Coco Gauff Beats Martina Trevisan to Clinch Spot in 2022 French Open Women's Final|first=Adam|last=Wells|date=2 June 2022|access-date=2 June 2022|website=[[Bleacher Report]]}}</ref> 17-year-old [[Linda Nosková]] was the youngest qualifier to debut in the main draw since [[Michelle Larcher de Brito]] in [[2009 French Open – Women's singles|2009]].<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.tennisnow.com/Blogs/NET-POSTS/May-2022/Girls-Champion-Noskova,-17,-Qualifies-for-Roland.aspx | title=Girls' Champion Noskova, 17, Qualifies for Roland Garros }}</ref> This marked only the second time in the [[Open Era]] when just one of the top ten seeds reached the fourth round of a major, after the [[2018 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|2018 Wimbledon Championships]].<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.rolandgarros.com/en-us/article/numbers-behind-week-one-at-rg2022 | title=Numbers behind week one at RG2022 - Roland-Garros - the 2022 Roland-Garros Tournament official site }}</ref>

This marked the final French Open appearance for [[2018 French Open – Women's singles|2018]] champion and former world No. 1 [[Simona Halep]]. She lost to [[Zheng Qinwen]] in the second round.

This was the first edition of the tournament to feature a final-set tiebreak.<ref>{{Cite web|title=The End of the Endless Final Set: Grand Slams Adopt Same Tiebreaker|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2022/03/17/sports/tennis/grand-slams-tiebreaker.html|first=Christopher|last=Clarey|date=17 March 2022|access-date=24 May 2022|work=[[The New York Times]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220509172915/https://www.nytimes.com/2022/03/17/sports/tennis/grand-slams-tiebreaker.html|archive-date=9 May 2022}}</ref> When the score in a final set reached 6–6, the first player to reach 10 points and lead by at least two points won the set (and the match). The first women's singles main-draw match to feature the ten-point tie break was the first-round match between [[Irina-Camelia Begu]] and [[Jasmine Paolini]], with Begu emerging victorious.
