{{main|2021 US Open (tennis)}}

[[Emma Raducanu]] defeated [[Leylah Fernandez]] in the final, 6–4, 6–3 to win the women's singles tennis title at the [[2021 US Open (tennis)|2021 US Open]]. It was her first [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] title. Raducanu became the first [[Qualifier (tennis)|qualifier]] to win a major. She was the first British woman to win a singles major since [[Virginia Wade]] at the [[1977 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|1977 Wimbledon Championships]], and the second player to win the US Open on her tournament debut (after [[Bianca Andreescu]] in [[2019 US Open – Women's singles|2019]]).<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2021/sep/08/emma-raducanu-marches-into-us-open-semi-finals-with-easy-win-over-bencic|title=Emma Raducanu marches into US Open semis with easy win over Bencic|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|date=8 September 2021|access-date=9 September 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2021/sep/09/emma-raducanu-maria-sakkari-leylah-annie-fernandez-us-open-tennis-semi-final|title=Emma Raducanu roars past Sakkari to set up US Open final against Fernandez|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|date=10 September 2021|access-date=10 September 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2021/sep/11/emma-raducanu-makes-tennis-history-with-us-open-final-win|title=Emma Raducanu: British 18-year-old makes tennis history with US Open final win|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|date=12 September 2021|access-date=14 September 2021}}</ref> Aged 18, Raducanu was the youngest major champion since [[Maria Sharapova]] at the [[2004 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|2004 Wimbledon Championships]], and with a [[WTA rankings|ranking]] of world No. 150, the lowest-ranked player to win a major since [[Kim Clijsters]] at the [[2009 US Open – Women's singles|2009 US Open]]. She [[List of Grand Slam–related tennis records#Won a title without losing a set|did not lose a set during the tournament]], including during her three qualification matches, and was not taken to a tiebreak in any set. It was her first [[WTA Tour]]-level singles title, making her the fourth woman in the [[Open Era]] to win a major as her first singles title. Raducanu won the title on only her second major main-draw appearance, an Open Era record.

[[Naomi Osaka]] was the defending champion,<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2020/sep/12/naomi-osaka-fights-back-to-sink-victoria-azarenka-and-win-us-open|title=Naomi Osaka fights back to sink Victoria Azarenka and regain US Open|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|date=12 September 2020|access-date=14 September 2020}}</ref> but was defeated by Fernandez in the third round.

The final marked the first all-teenage major final since [[Serena Williams]] defeated [[Martina Hingis]] at the [[1999 US Open – Women's singles|1999 US Open]], and the first women's singles major final in the Open Era to feature two unseeded players.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2247818/raducanu-makes-history-with-win-over-sakkari-sets-all-teen-us-open-final-vs-fernandez|title=Raducanu makes history with win over Sakkari; sets all-teen US Open final vs. Fernandez|website=wtatennis.com}}</ref> Raducanu and Fernandez both made their top 30 debuts following the tournament. Fernandez was the youngest player to defeat three top-five seeded players in the same major since Williams at the 1999 US Open.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2247811/fearless-fernandez-battles-past-sabalenka-into-first-grand-slam-final-at-us-open|title=Fearless Fernandez battles past Sabalenka into first Grand Slam final at US Open|website=wtatennis.com|access-date=12 September 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/58533341|title=Emma Raducanu wins US Open by beating Leylah Fernandez for maiden Grand Slam|publisher=BBC Sport|date=12 September 2021|access-date=14 September 2021}}</ref> She was the first player of Southeast Asian descent ([[Filipinos|Filipino]]) to reach the final.

This was the first major since the [[2011 French Open – Women's singles|2011 French Open]] and first US Open since [[2003 US Open – Women's singles|2003]] where neither of the [[Williams sisters]] participated.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://nypost.com/2021/08/25/venus-williams-joins-serena-in-withdrawing-from-us-open/|title=Venus Williams joins Serena in withdrawing from US Open: 'Not the best news'|newspaper=[[New York Post]]|date=25 August 2021|access-date=26 August 2021}}</ref>

None of the top 20 seeds lost prior to the third round, the first time this occurred since the introduction of the 32-seed format in [[2001 Wimbledon Championships|2001]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.usopen.org/en_US/players/seed.html|title=Players by Seed: Women's Seeds|website=usopen.org|access-date=3 September 2021|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200311101843/https://www.usopen.org/en_US/players/seed.html |archive-date=2020-03-11 }}</ref> The defeats of [[Karolína Plíšková]] and [[Barbora Krejčíková]] in the quarterfinals guaranteed two first-time major finalists; Raducanu and Fernandez emerged to become those players. This also ensured that, for the first time since [[List of Open Era Grand Slam women's singles finals|2014]], eight different players contested the four major finals in a season.

This marked the final US Open appearance of [[2016 US Open – Women's singles|2016]] champion and former world No. 1 [[Angelique Kerber]]; she lost to Fernandez in the fourth round. Kerber subsequently went on maternity leave in 2023 and retired from professional tennis in 2024.
