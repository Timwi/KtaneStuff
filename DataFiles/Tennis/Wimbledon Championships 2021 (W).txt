{{main article|2021 Wimbledon Championships}}

[[Ashleigh Barty]] defeated [[Karolína Plíšková]] in the final, 6–3, 6–7<sup>(4–7)</sup>, 6–3 to win the ladies' singles tennis title at the [[2021 Wimbledon Championships]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2021/jul/10/ashleigh-barty-karolina-pliskova-wimbledon-final-match-report|title=Ashleigh Barty battles past Karolina Pliskova to clinch first Wimbledon title|date=10 July 2021|work=Guardian|accessdate= 13 July 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/57783918|title=Ashleigh Barty beats Karolina Pliskova to win title|date=10 July 2021|work=BBC Sport|accessdate= 13 July 2021}}</ref> It was her first [[Wimbledon Championships|Wimbledon]] title and second [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] singles title overall. Barty was the first Australian to win the title since [[Evonne Goolagong Cawley]] in [[1980 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|1980]], and the fifth player (following [[Karen Susman]], [[Ann Jones (tennis)|Ann Jones]], [[Martina Hingis]] and [[Amélie Mauresmo]]) to win the women's singles title after previously winning the girls' singles title. She retained the [[List of WTA number 1 ranked tennis players#Singles|world No. 1 singles ranking]] by reaching the semifinals, with [[Aryna Sabalenka]] also in contention for the top ranking.

[[Simona Halep]] was the reigning champion from when the tournament was last held in [[2019 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|2019]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cnn.com/2019/07/13/tennis/simona-halep-serena-williams-wimbledon-final-tennis-intl-spt/index.html|title=Flawless Simona Halep beats Serena Williams to win first Wimbledon|website=CNN|date=14 July 2019}}</ref> but withdrew before the tournament due to a calf injury.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.wimbledon.com/en_GB/news/articles/2021-06-25/2021-06-25_halep_withdraws_from_championships_2021.html |title=Halep withdraws from Championships 2021 |access-date=2021-06-25 |website=www.wimbledon.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|date=June 25, 2021|title=Wimbledon 2021: Defending champion Simona Halep pulls out with calf injury|url=https://www.indiatoday.in/sports/tennis/story/simona-halep-pulls-out-of-wimbledon-2021-calf-injury-world-number-3-1819315-2021-06-25|access-date=2021-06-25|website=India Today|language=en}}</ref>

[[Serena Williams]] retired from her first-round match against [[Aliaksandra Sasnovich]] due to injury.<ref>{{Cite web|title='Heartbroken' Serena forced to retire from Wimbledon opener after injury|url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2180994/heartbroken-serena-forced-to-retire-from-wimbledon-opener-after-injury|access-date=2021-07-05|website=Women's Tennis Association|language=en}}</ref> This marked the first time since [[2014 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|2014]] that the Wimbledon final did not feature one of the [[Williams sisters]] (as [[Venus Williams]] lost to [[Ons Jabeur]] in the second round), as well as the first time since [[1997 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|1997]] that neither sister progressed beyond the second round.<ref>{{cite web|work=wimbledon.org|publisher=[[All England Lawn Tennis and Croquet Club]]|title=Ladies' Singles| url =http://www.wimbledon.com/en_GB/draws_archive/champions/ladiessingles.html| access-date =2021-07-07}}</ref>

This was the first edition of Wimbledon since [[2009 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|2009]] that saw the top two seeds progress to the semifinals.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/adamzagoria/2021/07/06/top-seeds-ash-barty-aryna-sabalenka-2018-champion-angelique-kerber-advance-to-wimbledon-semifinals/|title=Top Seeds Ash Barty, Aryna Sabalenka, 2018 Champion Angelique Kerber Advance to Wimbledon Semifinals|work=[[Forbes]]|last1=Zagoria|first1=Adam|date=6 July 2021|accessdate=7 July 2021}}</ref> Additionally, this was the first major in the [[Open Era]] where two wildcards, [[Liudmila Samsonova]] and [[Emma Raducanu]] (making her first major appearance), reached the fourth round.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://twitter.com/WTA_insider/status/1411442065485885443|title=WTA Insider on Twitter|date=3 July 2021|accessdate=3 July 2021}}</ref> Raducanu was the youngest British woman to reach the fourth round at Wimbledon in the Open Era.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2021/jul/03/emma-raducanu-wimbledon-2021-dream-goes-on-after-win-over-sorana-cirstea-tennis|title=Emma Raducanu's Wimbledon dream goes on after win over Sorana Cîrstea|last1=Cambers|first1=Simon|work=[[The Guardian]]|date=3 July 2021|accessdate=3 July 2021}}</ref> Jabeur was the first Tunisian and the first Arab woman to reach the Wimbledon quarterfinals.<ref>{{cite web|work=espn.com.au|publisher=[[ESPN]]|title=Ons Jabeur continues trailblazing Wimbledon run|date=5 July 2021| url=https://www.espn.com.au/tennis/story/_/id/31764968/ons-jabeur-continues-trailblazing-wimbledon-run|access-date=2021-07-11}}</ref>

This tournament marked the first Wimbledon main-draw appearances of [[2022 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|future]] [[2024 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|champions]] [[Elena Rybakina]] and [[Barbora Krejčíková]]. Both lost in the fourth round, to Sabalenka and Barty respectively.
