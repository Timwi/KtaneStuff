{{main|2021 French Open}}

[[Barbora Krejčíková]] defeated [[Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova]] in the final, 6–1, 2–6, 6–4 to win the women's singles tennis title at the [[2021 French Open]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/57455312|title=French Open: Barbora Krejcikova wins first Grand Slam singles title in Paris|date=12 June 2021|work=BBC Sport|accessdate= 18 June 2021}}</ref> It was her first [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] singles title.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2021/jun/12/barbora-krejcikova-beats-anastasia-pavlyuchenkova-to-win-french-open-title|title=Barbora Krejcikova beats Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova to win French Open|date=12 June 2021|work=Guardian|accessdate= 18 June 2021}}</ref>
Krejčíková [[List of Grand Slam–related tennis records#Won a title after saving match points|saved a match point en route to the title]], in the semifinals against [[Maria Sakkari]]. Krejčíková and Pavlyuchenkova were the combined lowest-ranked major finalists (world Nos. 33 and 32, respectively) since the introduction of the computer [[WTA rankings|rankings]] in 1975 (a feat surpassed just three months later at the [[2021 US Open – Women's singles final|US Open]]).

Krejčíková was the first Czech to win the title since [[Hana Mandlíková]] in [[1981 French Open – Women's singles|1981]].{{efn|Although [[Martina Navratilova]] was the women's singles champion at the French Open in [[1982 French Open – Women's singles|1982]] and [[1984 French Open – Women's singles|1984]], she won both titles whilst competing for the United States of America.}} She was also the first player to win both the singles and doubles titles at the same major since [[Serena Williams]] at the [[2016 Wimbledon Championships]], and the first to do so at the [[French Open]] since [[Mary Pierce]] in [[2000 French Open|2000]]. Pavlyuchenkova reached her first major final after participating in 52 major main draws, breaking the record previously set by [[Flavia Pennetta]] in the [[2015 US Open – Women's singles|2015 US Open]].<ref>{{cite news|title=Pavlyuchenkova bests Zidansek at French Open to make first Grand Slam final at 52nd attempt|url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2169169/pavlyuchenkova-bests-zidansek-at-french-open-to-make-first-grand-slam-final-at-52nd-attempt|publisher=WTA Tennis|access-date=10 June 2021|date=10 June 2021}}</ref>

[[Iga Świątek]] was the defending champion, but lost to Sakkari in the quarterfinals. Świątek's loss marked the 14th consecutive year where the reigning French Open champion failed to defend their title, with [[Justine Henin]] being the last woman to do so in [[2007 French Open – Women's singles|2007]] (Świątek would end this stretch herself [[2023 French Open – Women's singles|two years later]]).<ref>{{cite web|last=James|first=Dave|date=10 June 2021|title=Nadal braced for [[Ashleigh Barty]] and [[Naomi Osaka]] were in contention for the [[List of WTA number 1 ranked tennis players#Singles|world No. 1 singles ranking]]. Barty retained the top ranking after Osaka withdrew from the second round.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Smale|first1=Simon|date=4 June 2021|title=What now for Ash Barty after French Open exit?|url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2021-06-04/what-now-for-ash-barty-after-french-open-exit/100190222|work=[[ABC News (Australia)|ABC News]]|accessdate=6 June 2021}}</ref>

Serena Williams was the oldest woman in the Open Era to reach the fourth round; this was also the three-time champion's final French Open appearance before retiring from professional tennis the following year.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Busbee|first=Jay|title=Brady, Mickelson...Serena? The [[2019 WTA Tour#Points distribution|defending points]] from [[2019 Rosmalen Grass Court Championships – Women's singles|'s-Hertogenbosch]] is not included towards on dropping points.

As a result of pandemic-related adjustments to the ranking system and changes to the WTA Tour calendar in 2020, players will have the following potential adjustments to their ranking points after the tournament:
* Players who are defending points from the [[2019 French Open|2019 tournament]] in which are higher will be dropped on 14 June 2021; those points will be replaced by the higher of their points from 2019 or 2020 (points from 2020 will still be valid at the end of the 2021 tournament because the [[2020 French Open|2020 tournament]] delayed to four months in the normal scheduled calendar year; both 2019 and 2020 points are equaled within the same result will also be dropped on 11 October 2021).
* Players who are defending points from the 2020 tournament will have those points replaced by 2021 points only if the latter are higher.
* Players who have points from the 2020 tournament still counting towards their ranking on 14 June 2021 will have those points dropped on 11 October 2021 (52 weeks after the 2020 tournament); any 2020 points will be replaced by 2021 points at that time.
* Players who are not defending points from either the 2019 or 2020 tournaments will have their 16th best result replaced by their points from the 2021 tournament.

Note that this is a different ranking adjustment system than the one being used by the ATP for the men's event.
