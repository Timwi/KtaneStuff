{{further|2020 US Open (tennis)}}

[[Dominic Thiem]] defeated [[Alexander Zverev]] in the final, 2–6, 4–6, 6–4, 6–3, 7–6<sup>(8–6)</sup> to win the men's singles tennis title at the [[2020 US Open (tennis)|2020 US Open]]. It was his first and only [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] title, following three previous runner-up finishes. Thiem was the first man to come back from two sets down in a [[US Open (tennis)|US Open]] final in the [[Open Era]], the first to do so overall since [[1949 U.S. National Championships – Men's singles|1949]], and the first to do so in any major final since [[Gastón Gaudio]] at the [[2004 French Open – Men's singles|2004 French Open]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Fendrich|first=Howard|url=https://apnews.com/3772d6c78ba097ab1fc90aa29a934484|title=Thiem 1st since 1949 to win US Open after ceding 1st 2 sets|publisher=Associated Press|date=13 September 2020|access-date=14 September 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2020/sep/13/us-open-mens-final-2020-dominic-thiem-alexander-zverev|title =Dominic Thiem wins US Open final on tiebreak against Alexander Zverev after five-set thriller|date= 13 September 2020 |work=Guardian|access-date=14 September 2020}}</ref> Both players served for the championship in the final set (Zverev at 5–3 and Thiem at 6–5), but both were broken at 30. This was the first time that the US Open title was decided by a fifth set tie-break, 50 years after the rule was introduced in [[1970 US Open (tennis)|1970]].<ref name = "Thiem title">{{cite news|url=https://www.atptour.com/en/news/thiem-zverev-us-open-2020-final|title=Thiem Outlasts Zverev In Historic US Open Final|newspaper=ATP Tour}}</ref>

Thiem's victory made him the first Austrian to win a singles major since [[Thomas Muster]] at the [[1995 French Open – Men's singles|1995 French Open]], the first man born in the 1990s to win a singles major, and the first new male major singles champion since [[Marin Čilić]] won the [[2014 US Open – Men's singles|2014 US Open]].<ref name = "Thiem title" /> The gap between Čilić and Thiem's titles was the longest between two new major singles champions in the history of men's tennis, surpassing the previous longest gap between [[Juan Martín del Potro]] and [[Andy Murray]] respectively at the [[2009 US Open – Men's singles|2009]] and [[2012 US Open – Men's singles|2012]] editions of the US Open. It was also Thiem's 17th and last career [[ATP Tour]] title. Thiem remains the last player to date with a [[Backhand#One-handed backhand|one-handed backhand]] to win a major singles title.

Zverev was the youngest man to reach a major final since [[Novak Djokovic]] at the [[2010 US Open – Men's singles|2010 US Open]], and the first German to reach a major final since [[Rainer Schüttler]] at the [[2003 Australian Open – Men's singles|2003 Australian Open]].<ref name = "Thiem title" />
This was the first (and thus far only) men's final at any major to be contested by two players born in the 1990s.

[[Rafael Nadal]] was the reigning champion, but chose not to participate due to both safety concerns related to the ongoing [[COVID-19 pandemic]] and the short amount of time between the US Open and the rescheduled European [[clay court]] season.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/live/2019/sep/08/us-open-mens-final-rafael-nadal-v-daniil-medvedev-live |title =Rafael Nadal beats Daniil Medvedev to win US Open men's final – as it happened|date=8 September 2019|work=Guardian|access-date=10 September 2019}}</ref><ref name="washingtonpost.com">{{cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/2020/08/04/rafael-nadal-us-open/|title=Rafael Nadal bows out of U.S. Open, citing travel concerns amid pandemic|newspaper=Washington Post}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://tennishead.net/rafael-nadal-explains-us-open-decision-admitting-going-from-hardcourt-to-clay-would-be-dangerous-for-me/ |title=Rafael Nadal explains US Open decision, admitting: 'Going from hardcourt to clay would be dangerous for me' |date=August 11, 2020 |publisher=Tennishead |access-date=September 6, 2020 }}</ref> This marked the first time since the [[1999 US Open – Men's singles|1999 US Open]] that both Nadal and [[Roger Federer]] (who was recovering from a knee surgery) were absent from a major.<ref>{{cite web |title=Federer Announces Right Knee Procedure, To Return In 2021 |url=https://www.atptour.com/en/news/federer-procedure-announcement-june-2020 |website=ATP Tour |access-date=3 August 2020}}</ref>

There were no qualifiers this year. The [[ATP rankings]] valid on August 3 (being the rankings from March 16) were used to determine the main-draw entry list.

[[List of ATP number 1 ranked singles tennis players|World No. 1]] Novak Djokovic was defaulted from the tournament during his fourth-round match against [[Pablo Carreño Busta]]. After being broken to trail 6–5 in the first set, Djokovic frustratedly hit a ball towards the baseline and wound up hitting a line judge in the throat.<ref>{{cite news |title=US Open 2020: Novak Djokovic defaulted after hitting ball at line judge |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/54051920 |website=BBC |publisher=BBC Sport|access-date=6 September 2020}}</ref> Djokovic's unbeaten 26–0 [[2020 Novak Djokovic tennis season|2020 season]], as well as his 29-match winning streak (extending back to the [[2019 Davis Cup Finals]]), ended as a result. Djokovic was the first player to be defaulted from a major since [[Stefan Koubek]] at the [[2000 French Open – Men's singles|2000 French Open]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=US Open: Dominic Thiem beats Felix Auger-Aliassime to reach quarter-finals|url=https://www.skysports.com/tennis/news/32833/12066203/us-open-dominic-thiem-beats-felix-auger-aliassime-to-reach-quarter-finals|access-date=2020-09-09|website=Sky Sports|language=en}}</ref>

Djokovic's ejection from the tournament guaranteed a first-time major finalist in the top half of the draw. His disqualification also meant this would be the first major since the 2004 French Open not to feature any of the [[Big Three (tennis)|Big Three]] in the semifinals, the first major since the [[2003 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles|2003 Wimbledon Championships]] in which none of the quarterfinalists had previously won a major, and the first major men's singles tournament not to have been won by Djokovic, Federer or Nadal since [[Stan Wawrinka]] at the [[2016 US Open – Men's singles|2016 US Open]].<ref>[[List of Open Era Grand Slam men's singles finals]]</ref> Additionally, it meant this would be the first major won by a player under the age of 30 since the [[2016 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles|2016 Wimbledon Championships]]. For the first (and only) time in major history, all of the male quarterfinalists were born in the 1990s.<ref>{{Cite news|last=Culpepper|first=Chuck|title=With no household names left, the U.S. Open men's quarterfinals will feature some great unknowns|language=en-US|newspaper=Washington Post|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/tennis/us-open-mens-quarterfinal-draw/2020/09/07/58013e10-f12a-11ea-bc45-e5d48ab44b9f_story.html|access-date=2020-09-09|issn=0190-8286}}</ref>
