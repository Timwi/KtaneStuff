[[Carlos Alcaraz]] defeated [[Alexander Zverev]] in the final, 6–3, 2–6, 5–7, 6–1, 6–2 to win the men's singles tennis title at the [[2024 French Open]]. It was his first [[French Open]] title and third [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] title overall.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.atptour.com//en/news/alcaraz-roland-garros-2024-champion-feature|title= Alcaraz: 'It doesn't matter what I've achieved if I now stand still'|date=10 June 2024|accessdate=10 June 2024 |website=ATPtour.com}}</ref> At {{Age in years, months and days|2003|5|5|2024|5|26}} old, Alcaraz was the youngest man to win major titles on three different surfaces, having won the [[2022 US Open – Men's singles|2022 US Open]] on [[hardcourt]]s and [[2023 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles|2023 Wimbledon Championships]] on [[Grass court|grass]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rolandgarros.com/en-us/article/rg2024-alcaraz-outlasts-zverev-for-first-roland-garros-crown|title=Alcaraz outlasts Zverev for first Roland Garros crown|date=9 June 2024|website=rolandgarros.com|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Eccleshare |first=Charlie |title=Surface mastery: How Alcaraz won Grand Slams on hard, grass and clay courts |url=https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5553267/2024/06/11/carlos-alcaraz-three-grand-slams-surfaces/ |access-date=2024-06-12 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> He was the first French Open champion outside of [[Rafael Nadal]] or [[Novak Djokovic]] since [[Stan Wawrinka]] in [[2015 French Open – Men's singles|2015]]. Zverev was the first German to contest the final since [[Michael Stich]] in [[1996 French Open – Men's singles|1996]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Oddo |first=Chris |date=2024-06-07 |title=Zverev breaks through to first Paris final – Roland-Garros |url=https://www.rolandgarros.com/en-us/article/rg2024-zverev-breaks-through-to-maiden-major-final |access-date=2024-06-15 |website=www.rolandgarros.com |language=en-us}}</ref> 

Djokovic was the defending champion, but withdrew from his quarterfinal match due to a knee injury.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Carayol |first1=Tumaini |title=Novak Djokovic reaches record 23 grand slam titles after French Open final win |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2023/jun/11/novak-djokovic-casper-ruud-french-open-23-grand-slam-titles-tennis |access-date=26 April 2024 |work=The Guardian |date=11 June 2023}}</ref> His third-round match against [[Lorenzo Musetti]] marked the latest finish in tournament history, ending at 3:07 a.m. local time.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Djokovic digs deep to survive in five – Roland-Garros – The official site |url=https://www.rolandgarros.com/en-us/article/rg2024-musetti-djokovic-third-round |access-date=2024-06-05 |website=www.rolandgarros.com |language=en-us}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Berkok |first=John |date=1 June 2024 |title=Novak Djokovic on the brink of even more history after grueling win over Musetti at Roland Garros |url=https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/novak-djokovic-on-the-brink-of-even-more-history-after-grueling-win-over-musetti-at-roland-garros |accessdate=1 June 2024 |website=Tennis.com}}</ref> [[Jannik Sinner]] attained the [[List of ATP number 1 ranked singles tennis players|world No. 1 singles ranking]] for the first time following the tournament, becoming the first Italian to claim a top singles spot.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.atptour.com/en/news/sinner-world-no-1-hometown-visit|title=Home, sweet home: Sinner celebrates World No. 1 in 'special' hometown visit|date=11 June 2024|accessdate=13 June 2024|website=ATPtour.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Jannik Sinner assured of rise to World No. 1 {{!}} ATP Tour {{!}} Tennis |url=http://www.atptour.com/en/news/sinner-roland-garros-2024-world-no-1-guaranteed |access-date=2024-06-04 |website=ATPtour.com |language=en}}</ref>

This marked the last major appearance of 14-time French Open champion and 22-time major champion Nadal; he lost to Zverev in the first round, ending [[Rafael Nadal at the French Open|his storied career at the French Open]] with a 112–4 win-loss record. Nadal was unseeded at a major for the first time since the [[2005 Australian Open – Men's singles|2005 Australian Open]] while using a protected ranking to enter the main draw.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.npr.org/2024/05/24/nx-s1-4975897/14-time-french-open-champion-rafael-nadal-goes-into-the-tournament-unseeded|title=14-time French Open champion Rafael Nadal goes into the tournament unseeded|publisher=[[NPR]]|date=24 May 2024|access-date=25 May 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Carayol |first1=Tumaini |title=Rafael Nadal loses to Alexander Zverev in what may be his French Open farewell |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/article/2024/may/27/rafael-nadal-alexander-zverev-french-open-first-round |access-date=28 May 2024 |work=The Guardian |date=27 May 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Fendrich |first1=Howard |title=14-time champion Rafael Nadal loses in the French Open's first round to Alexander Zverev |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/tennis/2024/05/27/french-open-5-27-2024-results-nadal/c2c1cf3a-1c2d-11ef-becb-2cf8dbfd9eb9_story.html |access-date=28 May 2024 |newspaper=The Washington Post |date=27 May 2024}}</ref>  This tournament also marked the final major singles appearance of former world No. 1, two-time Olympic gold medalist, [[2016 French Open – Men's singles|2016]] finalist and three-time major champion [[Andy Murray]]; he lost to [[Stan Wawrinka]] in the first round.
